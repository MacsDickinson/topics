# One library, many sources

Proposed for discussion and BDD sign-off. The existing [agreed plan](../../docs/plan.md) describes the single-root prototype. This proposal changes the boundary of a workspace. It builds on the [storage assessment](../../docs/persistence-assessment.md) and the [folder setup and review flow](../folder-setup/PLAN.md).

## Pilot decision

Start with the [Friday sync pilot](PILOT.md) in one existing workspace. Use the ordinary work-week sync to discover what Topics missed and measure the effort of correcting it. The two-folder registry below follows that experiment. The user endorsed the pilot direction on 2026-11-18. The revised [pilot scenarios](pilot.feature) are proposed for build sign-off.

## Who opens Topics, and why?

You are about to meet the Ledger team. Since your last visit, twelve meeting notes repeated Friday’s rollout date and one engineering decision moved it to Tuesday. Remembering to search for “rollout” is exactly the work Topics should do for you. Success means you see the change when you open the app, can explain why it matters, and can find the dated decision behind it. A second user need arises during the meeting: capture a commitment in your own words and leave with a useful action. We propose two separately reviewable deliveries below.

## Journey 1 · Find out what changed

**Today is the landing screen.** After an authorized import, its “Since your last visit” section might contain one entry: **Ledger · Rollout moved from Friday to Tuesday**. Under it: “Engineering recorded a support handover as the reason. Earlier meeting notes still say Friday.” Select it to open Ledger at the change, with the two dated passages one disclosure away. If the evidence only disagrees, the heading says “Two rollout dates reported” and offers a single question. Ordinary repeats are silent. The proposal uses an in-app digest; desktop push notifications are outside this milestone.

The topic page shows the current saved understanding and the proposed update together. Save the displayed change as a group, correct it, or leave it for later. A topic with an unread change has a **New** indicator wherever its briefing is opened. Reading the update clears that indicator on both Today and the topic. An unsaved update remains available on the topic. It never confirms the assertion. A deferred question stays in the topic’s open questions and a quiet “For later” list. A materially different update can bring that topic back to Today. Opening Today triggers a local refresh. Show “No new changes in analysed sources” only when all selected inputs have been checked and processed. Otherwise show the affected source, its last successful check and whether it is checking, unavailable, failed, skipped or waiting for authorization. Offer one batch review when needed. Search by topic name or alias from any screen to return to its briefing, linked people, decisions, subtopics and original evidence. This is also how you find something that hasn’t changed lately.

**Sources** is where you connect a folder, see its last refresh or inspect a problem. “Library” names the local home for authored notes, saved knowledge and source registrations. It needs no navigation tab or all-documents landing page. Topics are the things you follow across those sources. Public-facing copy can say “Where to save your notes” when a folder choice matters. A project’s briefing can include lore attributed to a person, a dated decision and its rationale, and a concern shared with another project. Attendance alone never implies agreement.

**Connect once, review by change.** Choose a folder, preview one summary of the eligible inputs and authorize that exact selection for analysis. Full paths, exclusions and exceptions are available on demand. Existing local-only restrictions are honored automatically. Adjust scope when you need to. Saving a topic update takes one decision for the group. No per-document access questionnaire or requirement to mark every fact reviewed. If another version needs analysis consent, Today can offer one “Review changed sources” entry for the new batch. Simply discovering a file does not authorize sending it.

## Journey 2 · Turn my own note into a useful action

**Proposed next delivery, separately gated.** During the meeting, write “I need to ask Sam about support cover” in Today’s Daily Log and save once. Saving preserves the exact note in your Markdown home and triggers a proposed action beside it. With one relevant saved Ledger decision and an unambiguous Sam, the draft reads **Ask Sam about support cover** with Ledger, the Tuesday rollout and the dated decision attached as context. Tuesday is the rollout date; the action’s due date stays empty. You can accept, edit or dismiss the draft in place. Accepting creates one persistent action, visible from Today and Ledger. Weekly Review can carry that same action forward.

Use already permitted evidence for enrichment and show where it came from. Save the note locally even when analysis is unavailable. If a model needs fresh consent, offer one batch authorization. Where there are two Sams, leave the person unresolved and keep the suggested action useful. “Maybe Sam could ask about cover” is insufficient evidence of my commitment. Reopening or refreshing a note cannot create another copy of its action. See the [separate proposed capture scenarios](capture.feature).

## Persistence and compatibility

For a fresh library, keep authored Markdown and the existing app-owned `topics/` records together, with source registrations and versioned evidence sufficient to rebuild the derived index. Keep snapshots before writes and journalled undo. External sources are read-only. For an existing installation, offer the current root as the initial library home and preserve its record IDs, correction rules, history and citations in place. Existing source paths must resolve as before. Connecting a second source must never relocate those files or scan app-owned records as fresh evidence.

## Two-folder milestone after the pilot

**Deliver one loop end to end.** Connect two local Markdown folders, see what the second contributes to an existing topic, save the change as a group, and retrieve the cited briefing through Today and in-app search. This gives us an acceptance test for each subsequent connector.

1. Register a folder with a stable source ID and a human-readable label. Identify each document within its source. Identical relative paths in two folders are valid. Detect a repeated registration of the same canonical folder. Reject overlapping registrations in this slice with an explanation. Use source-version and passage identifiers for evidence.
2. On connect or refresh, inventory locally and show new, changed, missing and skipped inputs. Detect unchanged bytes and copied evidence. Apply the existing size and privacy limits. Obtain consent for the exact data before any model analysis. New versions require fresh consent. Persist a resumable proposed update per affected topic, with the previous understanding, new evidence and remaining uncertainty. Never merge people or topics solely on a shared name.
3. On **Today**, group changes by topic with a short explanation such as “Ledger rollout date changed” or “Two rollout dates conflict.” The user can follow a citation, save a coherent topic update, correct it, or defer the disagreement. One decision covers the displayed group. Accepting a sourced account does not personally verify every line. A correction survives future appearances of the same bad transcript.
4. Use in-app topic/alias search to open the same saved briefing reached from Today. Preserve the last-seen change per topic across restart. Keep seen state independent of approval, deferral and correction.

**MCP today.** The runtime creates an authenticated bridge scoped to a single analysis run. Codex can read its scoped sources and propose changes. The desktop’s stdio proxy forwards an existing session. There is no supported external client setup for searching saved topic briefings. Milestone one therefore ends at desktop retrieval. A later external MCP reader should share the briefing projection and have bounded read grants, revocation and filtering of private or derived-only material. Verify it with a separately launched client before promising agent access. See [runtime](../../crates/topics-runtime/src/lib.rs) and [proxy](../../crates/topics-runtime/src/proxy.rs).

**Recovery and removal.** A missing folder retains its identity and displays its last successful refresh. Removing a source stops future reads and invalidates its analysis authorizations. Preserve accepted history and the provenance of past decisions locally, label evidence availability accurately and exclude removed-source material from future model context. A later explicit purge of retained evidence is a separate user decision. Rebuilds must preserve privacy decisions and revocations.

## Sources beyond my notes

Prioritize the work systems for the evidence each can add. A **Confluence** decision page explains why Tuesday was chosen. A **GitHub** pull request can show the change was merged, with a link to the revision. A **Jira** issue can tell you which follow-up remains open and its recorded assignee. A merge alone is insufficient evidence that a rollout happened. Choosing the first work connector should depend on which missing answer matters most in the pilot. For example, restrict the first pilot to one chosen Confluence space, GitHub repository or Jira project, with a single source summary at connection time. Each connector needs its own plan for selected spaces/repos/projects, versioned citations, refresh, deletions and upstream access changes before cached results can be shown. ZIP snapshots, selected web pages and Git checkouts can use the same import lifecycle. Large inputs have their own [proposed plan](../large-notes/PLAN.md).

## How we will judge the benefit

These are proposed regression and usability targets for the broader milestone. The pilot’s field evidence and decision rule are in [Friday sync pilot](PILOT.md).

| Need | Evaluation through the product | Pass condition |
|---|---|---|
| Notice a material change without a search habit | Open Today after importing twelve redundant mentions, one superseding decision and one unrelated unresolved contradiction in synthetic folders. See the digest and deferral scenarios. | One entry per affected topic, correct direction and status for both, zero repeat-only entries. Reopening after review produces no repeat alert. |
| Understand why it changed | Give a first-time tester the synthetic workspace and ask “What changed for Ledger, why, and who recorded it?” Start at Today without coaching. Later ask them to find Ledger by alias when Today is empty. | They identify Tuesday, the handover and the cited decision in one minute on each journey. Record the route, time and any mistaken inference. A browser assertion alone cannot pass this task. |
| Keep control without bookkeeping | Connect a second folder with mixed sensitivity, then save a coherent update. Inspect the UI and the scripted provider’s received inputs. | One analysis consent for the exact selected manifest, including its internal batches and one save for the topic group, zero required per-file decisions, zero private inputs transmitted. See the consent, privacy and recovery scenarios. |
| Capture a useful action (next delivery) | Use the capture scenarios with an explicit commitment, a speculative mention, ambiguous people and a repeat save. Review drafts against a labelled synthetic answer key. | All cases yield the expected draft count, text, identity or unresolved state, and cited context. Zero fabricated deadlines, owners or duplicate actions. A user can accept the explicit commitment without retyping it. |

Run the applicable scenarios with temporary synthetic notes, isolated app data and a scripted provider. For model-dependent quality, also evaluate actual extraction on an explicitly authorized synthetic set before claiming the benefit. Record results and user-task observations against each row. For a miss, keep the milestone open, capture the missed case, revise and rerun until the agreed outcome is met. Agree the milestone-one [BDD scenarios](library.feature) before building. Capture, external MCP and each work connector have separate sign-off. Use the Topics design system for UI, Orca workers for build work and the required independent Claude review.
